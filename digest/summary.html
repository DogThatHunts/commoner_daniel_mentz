2. The argument in one page

All three talks tell one story in three layers.

  1. Status. Before the Civil War a person was a citizen of a state and held natural rights. Mentz says the 14th Amendment (1868) and the District of Columbia's incorporation (1871) added a second, federal-corporate citizenship on top, and that the old status survives under the statutory name "national" (01 [22:30], 01 [29:55]; repeated in 03 [06:25]). A person can declare it by affidavit and attach it to a passport application.
  2. Commerce. Mentz says the courts have run commercially since 1933 or 1938, so a traffic ticket is "a commercial instrument" with bonds behind it, and the practical response is to ask about jurisdiction and about the money, not to argue the traffic law (02 [08:52], 02 [1:28:00]).
  3. Community. What a free person does next is build: land, a counter-economy, a private realm, and a way of settling accusations that does not depend on the old institutions (03 [47:51], 03 [1:01:00]).

Three habits run through every layer. Consent is the mechanism: forms and officers keep asking "are you a citizen? a resident?" because the answer is a choice. Words with two meanings ("dialectics") hide that choice. And the recommended manner is honor and questions, not argument.

3. Where the three reinforce each other

  • Consent is the mechanism. The "two questions" ("Are you a citizen of the United States? Are you a resident?") are presented as the moment a person consents without knowing it (01 [35:32]). The system's designers keep exits open to "stay karmically clean," so the questions are about obtaining consent (01 [1:08:30]). A licence is a contract a person chooses to hold (02 [08:52]), and contracts ask the same two questions "because they know you have a choice" (03 [16:55]).
  • Ask questions, do not argue. The roadside is run as questions with no statements, "equity: a duty to inquire" (01 [1:24:18], 02 [37:20]). If it escalates, comply and say you are under duress, and "do not argue with the cops" (02 [1:18:34]). In court, "if you don't give them any controversy there's nothing left to administer" (03 [16:55]).
  • The old republic still exists, layered over. The Employment Development Department contract that accepts a "citizen or national" shows the old status "mirrored" inside the new one (01 [48:11]). Before the Civil War a private state citizen needed no licence (02 [17:46]). The "thesis of hope" in 03 makes the same point: the original republic "still exists" (03 [06:25]).
  • There is always a remedy. "There's always a way out" (01 [1:08:30]); "there's always a remedy" (02 [1:40:00]); four pathways in 03 (03 [06:25]).
  • The same four pathways appear every time, under slightly different names: national status, equity, the "trifecta" of church, trust and private membership association, and land patents or allodial land (01 [1:28:16], 02 [1:40:00], 03 [06:25]).
  • The passport card as the practical tool. Show the card, say little (01 [1:13:39], 02 [37:20]). Mentz says it carries a "do not detain" code (01 [42:02], 02 [57:45]).
  • The evidence is of one kind. Throughout, the support is on-screen exhibits and member stories: a sheriff on video, a pay stub, an unemployment claim, dismissed tickets. The "do not detain" code is described but, in Mentz's own words in 02, "we obviously don't have access to these records" (02 [57:45]). In 03 the claim becomes that sheriffs are "on board" and counties have "flipped" (03 [1:33:00]).
  • Each talk carries an offer. Courses and a membership call in 01 (01 [1:21:40]), a free complimentary membership in 02 (02 [1:40:00]), and the paid Exit and Build event and Fellowship courses in 03 (03 [47:51]).

4. Where the talks pull apart

T1. Do citations disappear, or do officers write them anyway? Medium Listed as a benefit of national status: the power to issue traffic citations, parking tickets and jury summonses "disappears" (01 [42:02]). Asked whether the vehicle code still applies, Mentz says "technically" it no longer does, "but the cops will not agree" (01 [1:13:39]). In 02 a member is arrested within seven minutes of a stop and a second is hit and charged (02 [1:09:45], 02 [1:21:00]). Mentz himself frames the gap as what is "technically" true against what officers will accept, and he says there is "no silver bullet" (02, summary section 1).

T2. "Looked for holes and found none," against "outcomes vary." Medium On the affidavit route he says he has looked "for all the years" for holes and found none (01 [29:55]). In 02 he stresses there is no silver bullet and that results vary by officer and court (02, summary section 1). The two can both be true if the first is about the paperwork and the second about enforcement, but the talks do not say so.

T3. Is there no income tax, or do the letters keep coming? High Benefits listed in 01: "no personal income tax, state or federal" (01 [42:02]). In 03 Mentz says the affidavit "opts you out of income tax without many issues, even state income tax" (03 [1:33:00]). Against that, a questioner in 01 says the IRS still says she owes, and the answer is to "rinse and repeat your documents" (01 [1:01:28]). In 03 Audrey sent paperwork and received IRS letters, and Mentz says the "notice of liability" that allies use is "not something we directly do inside the fellowship" (03 [1:21:35]).

T4. When did it change? Low The income tax is dated to 1913 (01 [19:10]) and the federal status to the 14th Amendment and the 1871 incorporation (01 [22:30]). The commercial turn is "technically since 1933," with 1938 for Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins (02 [08:52]) and "bankruptcy banking since 1933" (02 [1:28:00]). These can be separate layers, status and commerce, not rival dates.

T5. Stay in, or exit? Medium 01 lists "keep benefits (Social Security, veterans)" as a feature of national status (01 [42:02], 01 [1:17:05]). 03's slogan is "no voting, no violence, no apathy: exit and build" (03 [43:41–47:51]). Griess says exit and build does not need relocation, only a "security fence around your private world" (03 [1:12:00]), which fits keeping the benefits. But Mentz adds that people who bought land and kept paying tax were not really free (03 [1:12:00]).

T6. Other status groups. Low In 01 he says the Fellowship is using "most of their technique" of one other outfit, "refined" (01 [1:04:56]), and a slide says "American State National" does not exist (01 [51:40]). In 02 a member holds an "American state national" credential and he "can't speak to that method" (02 [57:45]). In 03 he says he has not reached out to those groups and has seen "more trouble than actual remedy" (03 [1:33:00]). These may refer to different groups.

T7. Certified mail "received and filed." Medium He says certified mail counts as received and filed even at a post-office box (02 [1:09:45]). In the same exchange Faye reports courts returning mail sent to P.O. boxes. The call does not resolve the difference.

6. Claims worth checking, across all three

These are observations for a public reader, not rebuttals. Each summary ends with its own list; this one picks out the points that recur.

  1. One definition carries a lot of weight. The statutory meaning of "United States" is taken from a definition whose own text limits it to its chapter (01, repeated in 03). Read the definition in full.
  2. The roadside script depends on "custodial." The question sequence assumes a stop that is not an arrest can be turned into a Miranda exchange. The case law cited in 02's summary treats an ordinary traffic stop as non-custodial.
  3. The case quotations need the full opinions. Where the opinion text was checked, one quoted sentence was not there (Belmont), one was cut short (Kitchens), and one precedent is generally understood to have been overruled by another the deck cites (Colgate by Madden).
  4. The outcome claims are anecdotes. "Forty-plus dismissals," charges "resolved by equity," counties "flipped," and the "do not detain" code are told, not shown, and the same calls contain accounts that went the other way (Faye and Irv in 02, Audrey in 03).
  5. Safety. Mentz's own advice is cautious. The only account of physical injury in the three talks is a member's own, and it follows his invoking the Fifth and Sixth Amendments at a stop (02 [1:21:00]).
  6. A few historical claims are contested. The "original 13th Amendment," and the derivation of "government" as "mind control," are flagged in 03's summary.
  7. Promotion. Courses, memberships and an event are described by their sellers in all three talks. The summaries mark where.
